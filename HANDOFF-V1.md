## 3 · Decisions locked since the log was written

### 3.1 · Ranking — decided 2026-10-06, applies to Stage 5 item 5

Ranking the corpus by score alone puts the trials the engine knows *least* about at the top:
`NCT05462704` (a pregnancy trial) and `NCT05759078` (post-myocardial-infarction) both score
**100 on 1–2 checkable criteria out of 11–20.** That is `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` §4a's
"confident-looking 87 built on 4 of 11 criteria" reappearing as a sort order.

**Decision: rank by `score × (decidable / total)`.**

```
sort key = score * (confidence.decidable / confidence.total)

  NCT06942208    88  |  6 of 12 checkable   ->  44.0    #1
  NCT07394972    75  |  4 of 14 checkable   ->  21.4    #2
  NCT05759078   100  |  2 of 20 checkable   ->  10.0    #7
  NCT05462704   100  |  1 of 11 checkable   ->   9.1    #8
```

**The two numbers stay strictly separate everywhere they are displayed.** Only the sort
order combines them. The UI still shows `88 / 100 match` and `6 of 12 criteria checkable`
side by side and visibly distinct, per Stage 5 item 3 — the combined value is never rendered
as a number anywhere. Blocked trials still sort last regardless.

**This is implemented.** `rankCorpus()` and a new `rankWeight()` in `index.html` sort on
score × confidence, blocked last. All 42 tests still pass. The order is now:

```
#1  NCT06851130   100  |  3 of 14      iron deficiency WITHOUT anemia
#2  NCT07394972   100  |  3 of 14      iron deficiency WITHOUT anemia
#3  NCT06894004   100  |  3 of 16      lipids, age 18-39
...
#5  NCT05759078   100  |  2 of 20      post-MI          (was #2 on score alone)
#7  NCT05462704   100  |  1 of 11      pregnancy        (was #1 on score alone)
7 trials blocked, sorted last
```

The two trials the profile actually matches on substance now lead the list.

**But one wrinkle Stage 5 must look at.** `NCT06942208` — the flagship iron trial, and the
one the whole demo is built around — scores **100 on 3 of 52 criteria** and lands around
**#9**, because its eligibility text is 7,789 characters of mostly prose. *Score × confidence
penalises a trial for publishing thorough criteria*, even when it is the correct match:

```
NCT06942208   PASS  34 years meets age 16–35 years
              PASS  8 ng/mL meets ferritin <= 50 ng/mL
              PASS  13.1 g/dL does not match hemoglobin < 12 g/dL
              43 of 52 bullets unparsed, 3 decidable -> rank key 5.8
```

All three decidable verdicts are correct and it is not blocked. The ranking rule is sound in
principle but the denominator punishes verbosity. Options for Stage 5: normalise confidence
against the corpus median rather than per-trial totals, or use decidable **count** rather
than ratio, or keep the rule and accept the order. **Do not quietly special-case the
flagship trial** — whatever rule is chosen has to be one that holds for all 23.

### 3.2 · Carried forward from Stages 1–4

- **`Rule.source` keeps the registry's markdown escaping** (`\<`, `\>`). That is verbatim as
  §4a requires. **Stage 5 must unescape at display time only** — leave `source` untouched —
  or the audit table quotes `\<` and it reads as a typo.
- **NEAR_MISS earns no score credit.** §4a says score is weighted PASS ÷ weighted decidable,
  so a near miss counts as a failure in the number and is surfaced separately in the amber
  callout (Stage 5 item 7). The old engine gave partial credit; the new one does not. That is
  intentional.
- **`presence` / `negation` rules all evaluate UNKNOWN** because the profile holds no
  condition data. 20% of rules are informative labels rather than checks.
- **Exclusion evidence wording was backwards and is fixed.** A passing exclusion rendered as
  `13.1 g/dL meets hemoglobin < 12 g/dL` — true that it passed, but it reads as the opposite
  of what happened. Exclusions are now phrased by what the value *does*
  (`does not match hemoglobin < 12 g/dL`), inclusions by whether they `meet`/`miss`. Found by
  reading real audit output, not by a test; **the audit table in Stage 5 is full of this kind
  of wording and needs reading with the same suspicion.**

## 4 · Open problems the next session inherits

These are real and were named rather than rounded up to done.

### 4.1 · Google Fonts is a live network call — **blocks Stage 7**

`index.html` lines 5–7 pull Bricolage Grotesque and Inter from Google. DevTools shows three
third-party requests on every load. No *application* request is made — no API, no
`smarthealthit`, nothing — but `CLAUDE.md` says **zero network calls at runtime** and Stage
7's six-rule audit includes "nothing sent anywhere". **This is currently false.**

Not fixed in a deletion stage because those two faces are the whole typographic identity and
the choice is self-host vs. fall back to system fonts. **Stage 7 has to decide.** Self-hosting
means base64-ing the woff2 into the single file, which is the only option that keeps both the
typography and the claim.

### 4.2 · The Health Score formula produces 99 and the number is soft

`docs/SIMPLIFY.md` §6 is implemented exactly as written. It yields **99 on a profile whose
ferritin is below range and falling**, because partial credit is measured against the width
of the reference interval and ferritin's is 138 wide — 4 units under the floor costs 2.9% of
its credit.

§6 itself warns a number that large and central "has to be defensible, or a judge reads it
as decoration". It wants **either per-marker weights or a tighter partial-credit span**, and
that is a `docs/SIMPLIFY.md` §6 revision, not a code change to make unilaterally. Weights are
already a real term in the implementation, currently uniform at 1.

### 4.3 · Still-fake content on Home — **V2 owns it, but it is on screen now**

`Records synced 18 new`, `Providers connected 5 of 5`, `Insurance: Full coverage`,
`Balance due $0.00`, and the bell's `18 new updates`. None is in V1's scope;
`docs/SIMPLIFY.md` §7 item 6 (hub shell, **V2**) owns that screen. Screen 2's per-marker
scale pins and its "3 of 4 markers in range" gauge are still typed percentages — true today
against `MARKERS`, but not computed.

### 4.4 · Parser granularity

When a block contains **no** checkable sub-bullet, the parser never learns that block's
sibling indent level, so a run of indented prose criteria folds into one unparsed rule
carrying several criteria in its verbatim source. Nothing is lost — all the text is shown —
but the granularity is coarser than ideal. `NCT07743983`'s exclusion block is the example.
