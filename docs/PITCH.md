# OpenHealth — Mock Shark Tank Pitch

> A **2–4 minute two-person pitch script** for Charlie and Liam, plus the offer sheet and
> Q&A prep behind it.
>
> **This is for a mock, in-school pitch panel. It has nothing to do with Congressional App
> Challenge judging**, which has no pitch or investment component. The valuation, the equity
> split, and the "sharks" are a classroom exercise.
>
> The *numbers*, however, are the real ones from [`COSTS.md`](COSTS.md) — so anything said
> on stage can be defended if someone asks where it came from.
>
> Speaking time is **deliberately equal**: Charlie 278 words, Liam 274 words. Every beat is
> labeled with its word count so the balance can be re-checked after any edit.

---

## 1 · How to read this

| Convention | Means |
|---|---|
| `> "quoted text"` | Say it close to verbatim. These lines are timed |
| `[KEY POINT]` | A point to make in your own words if there's room. Cuttable |
| *italic stage direction* | What your hands and eyes should be doing |

**Target runtime 3:25.** That leaves ~35 seconds of slack inside a 4:00 cap for a slow
demo or an interruption.

**Before you present:** the live app shows **Iron Revisited, 79/100, NCT06942208**. It does
**not** show "CGX Trial #4, 87/100" — that value in [`BRIEF.md`](BRIEF.md) predates the real
matching engine. Say **79**.

## 2 · The script

### Beat 1 · The hook — **CHARLIE** *(54 w)*

*Phone down at your side. Don't touch it. Let the number land.*

> "Eighty percent of clinical trials in this country miss their enrollment deadline. Not
> because nobody qualified — because the people who qualified never found out.
>
> Drug companies spend about a fifth of every trial's budget hunting for those exact
> patients.
>
> So we have patients who'd say yes, and sponsors desperate to ask. They never meet."

---

### Beat 2 · The problem — **LIAM** *(57 w)*

> "Here's why. If you're managing a condition in the US, your health data sits in five
> different places. Labs in one portal, prescriptions in another, billing in a third.
>
> Nobody sees the whole picture — least of all you.
>
> Trial-matching software does exist. But every product is sold to hospitals and pharma.
> The patient is last to know."

---

### Beat 3 · Why now — **CHARLIE** *(55 w)*

> "Three things changed recently. Federal law now requires your providers to hand you your
> own data through an API. Every recruiting trial in America is already a free public
> database. And AI can finally read eligibility rules written in plain English.
>
> All three landed inside five years. Nobody pointed them at the patient. We did."

*This is the first beat to cut if you're running long. See §6.*

---

### Beat 4 · Demo, screens 1–2 — **LIAM** *(55 w)*

*Liam holds the phone and taps his own screens.*

> "This is OpenHealth. Our patient, Jordan Reyes, is invented. **The trials are real.**
>
> **Screen one** — her whole record in one place. Eighteen updates, synced from five
> providers.
>
> **Screen two** — her bloodwork. Ferritin is eight. Normal starts at twelve. That's an
> anemia flag, and it's the thing that matters next.
>
> **Charlie — show them what it found.**"

*↑ That last line is the baton. Hand the phone over on it.*

---

### Beat 5 · Demo, screen 3 — **CHARLIE** *(73 w)*

*Take the phone, hold it up so they can see, then talk.*

> "**Screen three.** OpenHealth read her record against real trial criteria and scored it.
>
> **Seventy-nine out of a hundred** — 'Iron Revisited,' University of Calgary.
> **N-C-T zero-six-nine-four-two-two-zero-eight.** That's a real study. Look it up tonight.
>
> And it shows its work: every criterion she meets — and the one thing against her. The
> only site is two thousand miles away. We don't hide that.
>
> One tap sends her records to her own doctor."

`[KEY POINT]` There's a second trial in there scoring **21**. We show the bad match too.

---

### Beat 6 · Why us — **LIAM** *(58 w)*

> "So why hasn't someone done this? They have — pointed the other way. MatchMiner runs
> inside Dana-Farber. Deep 6 mines hospital records. All genuinely useful. All built so the
> *institution* gets the match.
>
> Every one of them sells to the people who already have your data. Nobody sells to
> **you**.
>
> We're the first one built so the patient does."

---

### Beat 7 · The business — **CHARLIE** *(64 w)*

> "We never charge the patient. Not a subscription, not a paywall, ever.
>
> We charge the recruitment budget that already exists. **Four hundred dollars per
> referral** — and only when a trial site actually *accepts* the patient. Fail screening,
> we earn nothing.
>
> That same patient costs a sponsor thousands today.
>
> Serving one user costs us **thirty-four cents a month** at scale. That's an **eighty-five
> percent margin**."

`[KEY POINT]` Billing on site-acceptance is deliberate — it makes an honest score a
*commercial* requirement, not just an ethical one.

---

### Beat 8 · The honest risk — **LIAM** *(75 w)*

***Liam, this is the strongest beat in the pitch. Slow down and hold eye contact.***

> "One thing before you ask it. Our biggest risk isn't the technology.
>
> Healthcare spends about **five hundred sixty dollars** to acquire a patient. Our
> economics support about **eleven**.
>
> So paid advertising can never work for us — which means we're not doing it. We go through
> patient advocacy groups that already have the trust and the audience. Antidote reaches
> fifteen million patients a month exactly that way.
>
> Distribution partnerships aren't our growth tactic. They're a **precondition**."

---

### Beat 9 · The ask — **BOTH** *(Liam 27 w · Charlie 30 w)*

**LIAM:**
> "The prototype is live right now. Real trials, real criteria, real scoring — not a mockup.
> We're asking **two hundred fifty thousand dollars for twenty percent** of OpenHealth."

**CHARLIE:**
> "That buys HIPAA compliance, one engineer, and a thousand-patient pilot with an advocacy
> partner — enough to prove the one number we can't prove yet: how many matches become real
> enrollments."

**CHARLIE:** "I'm Charlie." — **LIAM:** "I'm Liam." — **TOGETHER:** "This is OpenHealth."

## 4 · The offer sheet

### The ask: **$250,000 for 20%** → $1.25M post-money

Opening at 20% rather than an aggressive 10% anchor is deliberate: it is a realistic
pre-seed price for two students with a working prototype and no revenue, and it makes 25%
a *planned* concession instead of a retreat.

### Use of funds — 18-month runway to one answer

| Line | Amount | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| HIPAA compliance setup (policies, risk assessment, BAAs, legal review) | $30,000 | `[sourced]` real range is $10k–$30k |
| One contract engineer, 12 months | $95,000 | `[assumed]` |
| Pilot infrastructure, 18 mo @ $1,700/mo | $31,000 | `[modeled]` 1,000 users × $1.70 |
| Advocacy-partner pilot, 2 condition areas | $50,000 | `[modeled]` at blended CAC $15–$40 |
| Reserve | $44,000 | |
| **Total** | **$250,000** | |

All inputs from [`COSTS.md`](COSTS.md) §4 and §7.

### Negotiation ladder — agree on this *before* walking in

| | Deal | Post-money |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | $250k / **20%** | $1.25M |
| **Planned concession** | $250k / 25% | $1.0M |
| **Floor** | $250k / 30% | $833k |
| **If they say the round's too big** | $150k / 20% | $750k |
| **Walk away** | above 30%, or *any* deal requiring patient subscriptions | — |

**Concede in one step, not two.** If pushed, go straight from 20% to 25% and say *"that's
our last move on price."* Then hold. Two small concessions signal a third.

### The two scripted refusals

**If a shark demands 40%** — *Charlie delivers this,* since Liam has the other one:

> "That's more than we can do and still have two founders motivated for the three years
> this takes. We're at twenty-five. If that doesn't work for you we understand — but we'd
> rather have no deal than a broken cap table."

**If a shark pushes patient subscriptions** — *Liam delivers this:*

> "We'll give up equity. We won't charge patients. The patients who most need a trial are
> the least able to pay — and the moment they're the customer, we've become the thing we're
> replacing."

That second one is not a negotiating tactic. It's [`MISSION.md`](MISSION.md) §5, and it is
the one term that isn't for sale.

## 5 · Q&A prep — split so it stays even here too

### LIAM owns these four

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Why won't Apple or Epic just do this?" | Apple has consistently declined to make clinical recommendations — liability. Epic's customer is the hospital, and MyChart shows you *one* system's view. The fragmentation **is** their product boundary |
| "What's your revenue?" | "Zero. Pre-revenue, pre-pilot." Say it flat — hedging costs more than the zero does |
| "What if sites won't pay for a consumer-sourced referral?" | "That's our number-one kill risk. We test it with signed letters of intent *before* we build any billing" |
| "Isn't this a HIPAA nightmare?" | We're a patient-authorized conduit, not a provider. Every vendor touching PHI needs a signed BAA. $30k of this ask is exactly that work |

### CHARLIE owns these four

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "You're in high school. Why you?" | The matching engine is real code against the real registry, and we costed it against actual vendor pricing — Metriport, Epic, Aptible. We didn't mock up a business, we built one and stress-tested it |
| "What's the moat?" | Not the tech. The trust channel, and the honesty of the score — we surface criteria we *can't* determine, which is why a site accepts our referrals instead of filtering them out |
| "Biggest unintended consequence?" | All of someone's health *and* financial data in one place is a single bundle worth stealing. Concentration is the risk; controls reduce it but don't remove it. *(Also a class rubric item — answer it well)* |
| "Justify the valuation." | "Pre-seed digital health with a working product usually prices one and a half to three million. We deliberately came in **under** it. We'd rather have a partner who's genuinely in than win five percent on paper" |

`[KEY POINT]` if pushed further on price: *"We priced it so the conversation could be about
whether the business works, not about the number."*

## 6 · Day-of logistics

1. **One handoff, on Liam's line "Charlie — show them what it found."** Rehearse it five
   times. A clean pass looks choreographed; a fumbled one is the only thing anyone
   remembers.
2. **Second tab open** to
   [clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06942208](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06942208).
   If anyone says "prove it," you have it in two seconds. That moment can win the room by
   itself.
3. **Charlie: say the NCT ID out loud three times** in practice. It's the hardest thing in
   the script not to trip on.
4. **Say 79, not 87.** See §1.
5. **If you run past 4:00**, cut **beat 3 (Why now)** first — it's the most compressible.
   Then have Charlie pick up ~15 words elsewhere to keep §3 balanced.
6. **Neither of you looks at the phone while the other is talking.** One person presents,
   one person operates.
